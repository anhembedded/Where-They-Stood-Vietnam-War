# Editorial Standards and Research Policy

**Where They Stood: Vietnam War**

We make historical documentaries that let viewers follow the evidence behind the story. Each episode’s description points to its Research Notes and any published corrections.

| ID | Commitment | What viewers can check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ES-01 | Traceability | The Research Notes identify the source and page, section or archive record behind each major claim. |
| ES-02 | Source type | We identify contemporary records, field reports, testimony and later scholarship so viewers can judge each on its own terms. |
| ES-03 | Attribution | If an official or researcher relayed what someone else said, we identify the account as reported. |
| ES-04 | Limits | We state when a report covers one location, when a number is an estimate, and when the record leaves a question open. |
| ES-05 | Visual provenance | We credit archival images and label explanatory graphics or reconstructions where viewers could mistake them for a historical record. |
| ES-06 | Disagreement | When sources materially disagree, we explain who said what and whether differences in date, place or method affect the comparison. |
| ES-07 | Corrections | We accept documented feedback and log material corrections with the reason and the material updated. |

Where a reviewer contributed and agreed to be credited, we state their role, the material reviewed and any relevant relationship or financial interest.

## Visual labels

| On-screen label | What it means |
| :--- | :--- |
| **ARCHIVAL PHOTOGRAPH** | A historical photograph. The credit identifies its collection or record and, when verified, the date and location. |
| **EDITORIAL ILLUSTRATION** | A map, diagram or drawing made to explain documented material. |
| **HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION** | A recreated scene. The label accompanies the image when shown. Where footage or imagery is AI-generated, we identify it as such. |

## Corrections

If you see a factual error, please include the episode title, timecode and, if possible, a reference in a comment on the episode. For a material correction, we record the date, the original and corrected wording, the reason or new evidence, and which materials were updated in the [Corrections & Updates log](Corrections-and-Updates.md), linked from the episode’s Research Notes. If the original video still contains the error, the description should make the correction visible.
