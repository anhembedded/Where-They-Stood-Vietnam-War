# Episode Research Notes — Who Controlled the Village? (1963–1964)

**Where They Stood: Vietnam War · Episode 3**

## Episode overview

This episode examines what South Vietnam’s U.S.-backed Strategic Hamlet Program actually controlled between late 1963 and late 1964: why a province reporting `219` completed hamlets in September 1963 was estimated to have only `~45` meeting program standards two months later, why a drop in hamlets attacked (`77` in June to `27` in November) signaled weakening defenses rather than security, how optimistic May 1963 Pentagon curves (`~6,000` completed by April, `8,500` projected by July) collided with provincial budget incentives and post-coup governor turnover, what `1,250` Long An families told a wartime U.S. survey across three security subgroups, and what happened when Saigon’s renamed *New Life Hamlets* (`9 March 1964`) faced a `92`-day dismantling campaign at Bến Tượng (`May–September 1964`).

## Detailed quantitative record (moved from spoken narration for clarity)

To keep the spoken film focused on causal narrative rather than a spreadsheet, several verified secondary statistics appear in on-screen charts/maps or here:

- **Post-coup province-chief turnover (`1 Nov 1963 – Mar 1964`):** `35` of `41` sitting province chiefs replaced nationwide (`9` provinces changed governors `3` times in `3` months, `FRUS 1964 v01 d84`, p. 156); Long An specifically saw `6` province chiefs in weeks (`FRUS 1964 v01 d154`, p. 323).
- **Saigon’s internal November 1963 delta audit (`File 80/01`, National Archives Center II):** Bình Minh district (Vĩnh Long) stalled `100%`, Tam Bình `90%`; Vĩnh Bình stalled `50%` with `7` hamlets evacuated; Kiên Giang reported only **`37 / 197`** hamlets still held real value (*"because previously local officials did not dare report the true situation"*, Phạm Đức Thuận 2017, pp. 92, 100–101).
- **Provincial contrast (`1963–1964`):** Security was uneven rather than uniform nationwide. In An Giang (Hòa Hảo area), road movement remained possible without armed escort in March 1964 (`FRUS 1964 d84`, p. 156); in Bình Định, few hamlets were attacked and none overrun during the 12 months ending `30 June 1963` (though `75` were damaged in the winter of 1963–64); Quảng Ngãi dropped from `413` to `275` government-held hamlets (`335` damaged to varying degrees, `FRUS 1964 d84`); Cà Mau dropped from `405` (1961–62) to `< 40` by late 1963 and `14` by end of 1964; Đông Lợi hamlet (Phụng Hiệp, Cần Thơ) was dismantled and rebuilt `104` times (Phạm Đức Thuận 2017, pp. 86, 122).
- **Wartime measurement, chart-date & survey caveats:** Table 2 in *Pentagon Papers* Part IV.B.2 (`p. 47` PDF / `p. 31` internal) reproduces a `11 May 1963` Joint Chiefs of Staff report (*Trends in the Counterinsurgency Effort in South Vietnam*, p. 60, fn. 103, citing an overrun rate of **fewer than two-tenths of one percent (`< 0.2%`)**); its blank late-1963 and 1964 grid squares reflect the chart's May 1963 compilation date on a pre-printed template rather than a halt in reporting. Michael Forrestal’s `11 Dec 1963` memo (`FRUS IV/360`, p. 699) noted that part of the post-coup drop in reported hamlets could reflect *"a new look being taken by the new regime at an old program"* alongside Viet Cong infiltration and prior statistical exaggeration—explanations that could coexist rather than exclude one another. Likewise, USIA's `1,250`-family survey in Long An (`FRUS 1964 d90`, pp. 179–180) reported results across three distinct subgroups (`100%` pro-government in very secure villages with schools/wells; `50/50` mildly pro-government vs. Hilsman's `"plague on both their houses"` summary in relatively secure villages; `75%` opposed to both sides—summarized by Hilsman as `"a plague on both their houses"`—and `25%` gave no answer / recorded silent in **insecure villages**), gathered by U.S. interviewers under wartime conditions without itemizing exact household counts per subgroup.
- **Territorial, firepower & Bến Tượng caveats (`1964`):** McNamara’s March 1964 memo estimated `~40%` of South Vietnamese **land area / territory** under Viet Cong control or predominant influence (`90%` of the land area of Long An, Bình Dương, Định Tường, Kiến Hòa, Kiến Tường, `FRUS 1964 d84`, p. 155); under *New Life Hamlets* in 1964, artillery rounds in Sóc Trăng–Bạc Liêu rural zones rose `10x` and airstrikes `3x` compared to 1963 (Phạm Đức Thuận 2017, pp. 105–106). At Bến Tượng (`5–9/1964`), Huỳnh Thị Liêm (2006, p. 165) writes *"Trên 85 hộ gia đình"* (more than 85 households out of `201` relocated in 1962) returned to their former homes (`~40–50` remaining, p. 164), whereas another secondary summary cites `85%`; because that household vs. percentage discrepancy rests on a 1964 field memo not independently inspected here, the spoken narration describes families dismantling roofs and carrying timber home without stating `85`.

## Visual sources and evidence boundaries

- **Direct archival sources:** *Pentagon Papers* IV.B.2 chart (p. 46) and summary (p. 5); *Viet Nam’s Strategic Hamlets* (Feb 1963, p. 23, p. 37 — *"Reproduction authorized"* p. 48); Signal Corps Saigon (`CC27577`, 1964) and Bàu Trai (`CC43419`); NARA `652988` Lương Phú A segment.
- **Contemporary illustrative context:** Tuy Hòa 16mm footage (`NARA 28773/28774`, Sept 1962), unnamed NARA `652988` reels (`1963`), Signal Corps (`CC22529`, `CC22534`, `CC28082` Vĩnh Long Oct 1964), and USN `UH-34D` (`Nov 1964`, with designed rotor/air-pass sound disclosed). Each clip is labeled on screen with its actual archive origin and never presented as footage of Long An’s night ban or Bến Tượng specifically.
- **Uncertainty boundary:** Neither U.S. nor RVN files cleanly separate the quantitative share between hamlets overrun at night (`FRUS IV/352`) and paper hamlets inflated for provincial budgets (`FRUS IV/360`), and *Pentagon Papers* explicitly keeps its counterfactual verdict at *"case not proved"*.
